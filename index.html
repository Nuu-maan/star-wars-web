The Waiting World

Parts One to Three

Act I: The Waiting World

Scene one: Two Suns

A wide desert valley under two suns. Red cliffs frame the view on both sides. A small domed settlement sits below a line of distant mesas, and dust drifts across the floor of the valley.

The Waiting World

Scroll to begin

Two suns, one horizon, and Luke Skywalker still looking past it.

Before any of it went wrong, there was only the long dry patience of this place.

Scene two: The Settlement

The camera pushes in on the domed settlement, a huddle of pale buildings and antenna towers on the valley floor.

Everything he had ever owned fit inside one bend of the road.

Luke: “Another season. That’s all. One more and I’m gone.”

He had been saying it for four years.

Scene three: What Leaving Looks Like

The view tilts up from the valley to the sky, where the two suns hang close together. The dust thins.

Ships left every week. He could name all of them by their engine note.

The suns always set together. He watched them alone.

Scene four: Something Comes Down

A fast push toward the horizon. The light drops and the dust thickens over the valley floor.

The thing that changed it did not arrive like an omen.

It came down badly, three kilometres out, trailing smoke and complaining.

Krummp…

Luke: “…that’s not a ship.”

Act II: The Message

Scene five: Out of the Heat

Inside a homestead: thick curved walls, a sunken seating pit around a round table, and an arched doorway open on the sunset. Luke Skywalker stands to the left of the table.

Inside, the day gave up at the door.

Thick walls, cool air, and the particular quiet of a room built to outlast weather.

Scene six: It Would Not Say

The camera pushes toward R2-D2, a battered astromech droid, squatting on the round table. Luke leans over him.

He set it on the table and waited for it to decide he was worth trusting.

Luke: “You’re a farm unit. Farm units don’t have sealed buffers.”

It turned its lens toward the door and said nothing at all.

Scene seven: The Table Wakes

Close on the round table. An inset panel shows its emitter waking with a cold blue light. When the transmission plays, a woman in a failing hologram resolves above the table.

Vzzk—

The table woke before he touched it.

A cold blue thread climbed out of the emitter and waited to be told it was allowed.

…signal degraded. Reconstructing.

…if this reaches anyone still listening out here…

…they have taken the archive. What is left of it is on this unit.

Leia: “There is no one else. There is only whoever is standing there.”

Scene eight: A Route East

The hologram of Princess Leia stands resolved above the table. The camera eases back to take in the room.

The message ended the way honest pleas end — without a promise that anyone would come.

Then the light folded into a line of coordinates, and the line pointed east.

Luke: “…that’s eleven hours. In open desert.”

Act III: Into the Dune Sea

Scene nine: Before Light

A canyon road at first light. A rust-red hover skiff idles on the sand, and Luke stands beside it shading his eyes, looking down the canyon.

He left before either sun, so nobody could talk him out of it.

He took water, a fusion cutter, and a droid that still would not explain itself.

Scene ten: The Dune Sea Opens

The camera pulls back. Luke and the skiff are tiny on the road; the canyon walls frame a vast desert opening beyond.

The maps stopped agreeing with each other about here.

Beyond the canyon, the desert stopped being a place he knew.

Scene eleven: Obi-Wan Kenobi

Obi-Wan Kenobi, an old man in a brown robe leaning on a staff, stands in the middle of the road where it gives out. Luke faces him.

Someone was already standing where the road gave out.

Obi-Wan: “You’re carrying something that doesn’t belong to you.”

Obi-Wan: “Then you already know what it costs. Go on, or turn around. Both are honest.”

Scene twelve: Watched

Three Tusken Raiders stand small and high on the ridge, watching. Far below, Luke and the skiff are on the road.

They had been on the ridge for some time.

Curiosity had carried him this far. Something heavier would have to carry him the rest of the way.

End of Part One

Act IV: The Last Safe Light

Scene thirteen: The Port

Mos Eisley under two low suns. Sandstone domes, market canopies and antenna towers crowd toward the horizon, and a freighter sits on a pad at the far left edge of town. Signposts and a railing frame the view.

Part Two

The port was the only place on the whole world where the sky was for sale.

He arrived at dusk with the droid, the coordinates, and eleven hours of sand in his teeth.

Scene fourteen: Nobody Looks Twice

The camera pushes in on the control tower and the crowded street below it, lit by lanterns and doorways.

Nobody looked twice. That was the whole point of a place like this.

Dock hand: “Cargo or passenger?” “Both.”

The tower blinked its slow red blink over everything, counting nothing in particular.

Scene fifteen: The Held Breath

A close view of the freighter on the far pad, low against the sunset, its engines lit.

One freighter was still lit, low on the western pad, its engines idling like a held breath.

Hmmmmmm…

He knew its engine note. He had heard it leave a hundred times from the far side of the valley.

Scene sixteen: The Ordinary Things

A market stall on the way to the pad, hung with lanterns, rope, tools, and a battered radio. Then a wide panel: Luke, case in hand, walks away from us toward the freighter with R2-D2 rolling beside him.

On the way to the pad he passed a stall selling the ordinary things of home.

Lantern oil. Rope. A radio that only ever got one station.

Luke: “…one more season, he’d said.”

He did not buy anything. He looked for a long time.

Act V: Leave the Sky Behind

Scene seventeen: Somebody Else’s Ship

Inside a worn freighter cockpit. Two empty seats face a console of amber dials. Through the canopy, a pale planet hangs against the dark.

The freighter’s name had been painted over twice. He decided not to ask.

Inside it smelled of coolant and somebody else’s cooking.

Scene eighteen: Strap In

Close on the console: dials, toggles, and a cracked navigation display.

Pilot: “Strap in. She lifts rough and she doesn’t apologise.”

He had fixed a hundred consoles like this one. He had never sat behind one while it mattered.

Scene nineteen: The Ground Lets Go

The view swings to the left of the canopy, where the desert world falls away, small and gold.

Then the ground let go of him.

The world he had spent his whole life leaving turned out to be small, and gold, and already behind him.

Scene twenty: The Stars Go Long

Straight ahead through the canopy, a ring of blue light opens and swallows the view as the ship jumps.

The droid found the coordinates on its own and fed them to the nav without being asked.

Pilot: “Hold on to something that isn’t me.”

The stars went long.

VWOOMM

Act VI: A Colder World

Scene twenty-one: No Colour At All

From high above, the curve of a frozen world. Glacier cliffs and a walker on the skyline run to the horizon under a thin blue sky, and a ridge of blown snow rises in the foreground.

The ship dropped out over a world with no colour in it at all.

White below, black above, and a thin blue line between them that was all the sky it had.

Scene twenty-two: Here

The wide valley. Far off, a cluster of spires that could be rock or could be built.

The coordinates pointed at a valley like every other valley.

Then the droid said the first word it had said in eleven days.

R2-D2: “Here.”

Scene twenty-three: Not Ice

Close on the valley floor: hangar doors cut into the far cliff, and a smaller structure half buried in the snow, too regular to be natural.

There was something in the valley that was not ice.

…thermal: negative.

…structure: artificial. Buried. Old.

…movement on the ridge. Not ours.

Scene twenty-four: On the Ridge

An Imperial probe droid stands on the snow ridge, one sensor lit, turned toward the ship. Far behind it a walker crosses the skyline.

It had been waiting on the ridge for some time.

Pilot: “Tell me that’s local wildlife.”

It was not.

End of Part Two

Act VII: The Landing

Scene twenty-five: Nobody Lit the Pad

Sunset over an ice plain. The freighter drops through the storm toward a landing pad ringed with beacon towers; the Echo Base beacon flickers under the mountains behind it.

Part Three

The ship came down on a pad nobody had lit for them.

Beacons still burned along the approach. Whoever set them had not stayed to see who came.

Scene twenty-six: Not Staying

Close on the freighter settling onto the pad, landing lights on the snow.

The engines wound down into the kind of silence that has weight.

Luke: “We’re not staying.”

He said it to the droid. The droid did not answer, which was an answer.

Scene twenty-seven: The Ramp

A wide panel: Luke comes down the freighter’s ramp into blowing snow, R2-D2 following, his dome turned toward a thin amber pulse beneath the ice.

The ramp met the ice with a sound like a door closing behind him.

Wheeoo…?

R2-D2 followed, dome turning, and gave the worried rising whistle he saved for bad weather and worse ideas.

Scene twenty-eight: Sensing Something

Luke kneels in the snow connecting a sensor lead to R2-D2, whose dome is fixed on the amber pulse under the ice. High on the ridge behind them, a probe droid crosses the skyline.

Luke: “You’re sensing something?”

R2’s dome snapped toward the ridge. Luke only saw the probe when he followed it.

Scene twenty-nine: A Door in the Ice

The Echo Base entrance: blast doors cut into the glacier, lit by beacons, with cables and crates half buried in the snow.

Luke Skywalker stepped onto a world that had never heard of sand.

The coordinates ended forty metres ahead, at a door in the ice.

Act VIII: The Buried Signal

Scene thirty: Built Before the Base

A tunnel cut through blue glacier ice opens into a vast chamber. Old cables disappear under the floor. A round dais sits in the middle, and Luke and R2-D2 stand small at the left.

Inside, the wind gave up, and the cold got personal.

Luke: “This place was built before the base.”

Old cables ran under the floor and did not come back up.

Scene thirty-one: Eleven Seconds

Close on Luke and R2-D2 at the edge of the floor, looking toward the dais.

The amber pulse came again. Eleven seconds. Then again.

Luke: “You’ve been here.”

It did not deny it.

Scene thirty-two: The Scan

An ice-crusted console rises out of the dais. R2-D2 projects a blue scan across it that finds a geometric doorway hidden behind the ice ribs.

Vzzk—

R2 threw a blue scan across the dais. It found a doorway where the ice had only shown ribs.

…relay node. Sealed four years.

…key accepted. Unit: R2-D2.

Leia: “If you hear this, the war has reached you.”

…archive fragment 3 of 11. Transferring.

Scene thirty-three: Shut It Down

The doorway stands open one handspan. A star map burns in the air above the console, and Luke reaches toward it while R2-D2 holds the scan. The map ends on a red Imperial tracking mark.

The door opened one handspan. A star map burned across the chamber, and ended on a red mark that was not theirs.

Somebody had been listening to the relay longer than they had.

Luke: “R2… shut it down.”

Act IX: Before Dawn

Scene thirty-four: The Beam

Luke runs across the ice toward the freighter’s ramp, hauling R2-D2 by his restraining bar. Behind them a probe droid drops into the valley, its search beam sweeping the snow.

The probe dropped into the valley. Its beam found the door before it found them.

Luke: “Come on, buddy. Move!”

Scene thirty-five: The Ramp Closing

Close on Luke and R2-D2 at the foot of the ramp as it begins to rise.

WHEEP-wheep-WHEEP!

He took R2 by the restraining bar and ran for the ramp as it started to close.

Scene thirty-six: Amber on Blue

The freighter climbs away over the frozen valley, engines flaring amber against the blue ice, under a sky just beginning to warm.

R2 locked into the socket. The engines flared amber against the blue.

Below them the sky did something he had no word for. It was every colour the desert had never been.

Scene thirty-seven: Before Sunrise

The freighter, small now, clears the ridge and heads into the dawn. Somewhere below, the buried doorway is still open, its red mark still lit.

The ship climbed over the ridge and into the dawn. Below, the door stayed open one handspan, and the red mark went on burning.

Luke left Hoth before sunrise. The signal left with him.

End of Part Three

Act X: The Red Echo

Scene thirty-eight: A Red Light

In the freighter’s cockpit, still climbing out of Hoth’s dawn, Luke leans over the amber star map R2-D2 is projecting. A red point pulses on it and lights his hand. Beyond the canopy, TIE fighters rise over the ice.

Part Four

They were still climbing when he noticed the red on his hand.

Luke: “That red light isn’t a destination. It’s telling them where we are.”

Wheep! Wheep… brrrt.

Scene thirty-nine: The Pale Curve

Wide: the freighter clears the pale curve of the ice planet with a patrol of TIE fighters behind it, heading for an asteroid belt. Beyond the rocks, an abandoned relay station turns slowly, its great dish broken along one edge.

The planet fell away white and enormous. The fighters did not.

Luke: “We can’t take this signal to anyone else.”

Past the rocks, something old was turning in the dark, one amber lamp still burning.

Scene forty: Silent Running

Luke throttles down and cuts the transmitter. The cockpit goes dim as the freighter coasts behind a broad asteroid, R2-D2 at his shoulder.

He cut the transmitter and let the dark have them.

Luke: “That relay. If its systems still work, we can separate the map from the beacon.”

Scene forty-one: One Little Light

The fighters sweep past the asteroid, still hunting the last broadcast. The relay station waits beyond the rocks.

Wooo-oo…

Luke: “I know. I’m asking a lot of one little light.”

Act XI: The Silent Relay

Scene forty-two: Someone Kept the Lights On

The freighter rests in the relay’s pressurised hangar under warm lamps: an empty floor, old cables, and a circular hatch to the control room. The ice planet hangs beyond the viewing glass.

R2 talked a docking lock open. The doors sealed behind them and the air came back.

Luke: “Someone kept the lights on.”

Scene forty-three: The Keeper

In the control room, R2-D2 plugs into a round brass console. Amber refugee routes bloom into tiny civilian ships, with the red tracking point threaded through them. A blue recording of a tired Mon Calamari technician flickers above.

The console knew the map. It had been waiting for it.

Relay keeper: “If you hear this, the war has reached you. These routes were made for people who had nowhere left to go.”

Relay keeper: “Keep the routes. Leave the beacon behind.”

Luke: “It wasn’t calling us to a place. It was asking us to help.”

Scene forty-four: Together

The old power bus strains. Sparks spill from a cable and a heavy console cover slips; Luke catches it and kneels beside R2-D2, keeping the connection clear as the hatch lamp turns red.

WHEEE-OOP!

R2 was telling him to go. Luke had learned that whistle on Hoth.

Luke: “No. We leave together.”

Scene forty-five: The Echo

Luke rests a hand on R2-D2’s dome. The amber routes vanish into the droid’s memory; the red process stays behind on the relay console.

The amber went into R2. The red stayed where it was.

Luke: “Let them follow the echo. We’ll carry the way out.”

Act XII: A Borrowed Dawn

Scene forty-six: The Last Connection

R2-D2 pulls his connector from the red decoy console and rolls for the hatch. Luke holds it open; beyond it, the short passage leads to the freighter’s amber-lit ramp.

R2 left the relay singing their old signature to anyone who would listen.

Luke: “You’ve done it. Come on, buddy.”

Bweep! Brrp-brrp!

Scene forty-seven: Follow the Light

Wide: the freighter slips out behind the nearest asteroid while the relay’s broken dish burns red. The TIE patrol banks toward the station.

The dish lit red. Every fighter in the belt turned to look at it.

Luke: “That’s it. Follow the light.”

Scene forty-eight: No Red Light

Safely behind the rocks, Luke brings the engines up. R2-D2’s clean copy of the map glows amber between them, with no red mark anywhere on it. Blue streaks fill the canopy as they jump.

Luke: “No red light. Just the way forward.”

Wheee-oo-dee!

Scene forty-nine: A Way Home

In hyperspace, Luke rests his hand on R2-D2’s dome. The cockpit is warm and still around the two of them and the amber map.

Luke: “Let’s find the people who need it.”

They had left Hoth carrying a warning. Now they carried a way home.

End of Part Four
